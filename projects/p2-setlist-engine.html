Project 2 — Setlist Engine

A live visual instrument for a whole DJ set. Each track in the setlist gets its own scene, palette and title card. It listens to the mixer in real time, and you perform it from the keyboard during the set.

The brief

  • Get a real setlist (6–12 tracks) from your DJ friend
  • Build or adapt at least 5 scenes, at least 2 of them your own designs
  • Give each track a scene, a palette and a beat sensitivity, and show a title card when the track changes
  • It must run on live input (mic or line-in) and work fullscreen and offline
  • Include performance controls: next/previous track, scene override, blackout, strobe, tap tempo
  • Deliverable: a 60–120 second recording of a "mini set" plus the exported HTML (lesson 11)
Time budget: about 6½ hours The starter already has 6 working scenes and every control. Your job is to make it yours (scenes, palettes, typography) and fit it to the setlist. That's what makes this a portfolio piece.

Milestones

  1. Collect the set (20 min). Get the setlist from your DJ friend: title, artist, rough BPM and a one-word vibe for each track. Fill in SETLIST in the starter.
  2. Learn the controls (20 min). Run the starter, click to play, and try every key in the HUD. Then switch the track bar to 🎤 Mic / line-in and play music from your phone. Get a feel for it as an instrument.
  3. Scene #7: your own (75 min). Port your best lesson challenge (cover art, speaker wall, drop detector, starfield) into a new scene function and add it to SCENES.
  4. Scene #8: your own (75 min). Make something new. Ideas: a kaleidoscope (draw one slice and rotate copies), giant typography of the track title that pulses, a 3D-looking grid of noise lines, or a strobing barcode.
  5. Tune the 6 built-in scenes (45 min). Change at least something in each: shapes, speeds, how they react. Delete any you don't like. Fewer, better scenes win.
  6. Fit it to the set (45 min). Give each track a scene, palette and sensitivity that match its vibe. Build an arc across the set: calm openers, intense peak time, an emotional closer.
  7. Title cards & look (30 min). Style the title card (font size, position, timing). Consider a small persistent logo or the DJ's name in a corner.
  8. Rehearse (30 min). Play the actual set, or a mix, through live input. Practice switching tracks (N) on the transitions and using blackout and strobe on big moments. Note what feels wrong and fix it.
  9. Ship it (20 min). ⤓ Export, open the file with no internet, test "Use mic / line-in" and fullscreen on the laptop that will run the gig. Record a mini set following lesson 11.

Controls

KeyAction
N / BNext / previous track in the setlist (applies its scene and palette, shows its title card)
1–9Override the scene
PNext palette
TTap tempo: tap 4+ times on the beat to switch to tempo clock mode
GSwitch back to automatic beat detection
↑ / ↓Beat sensitivity
S (hold)Strobe
SpaceBlackout
EnterShow the title card again
F / HFullscreen / hide HUD
ClickPlay/pause (file mode only)

Architecture recap

  • SETLIST is data, not code. Changing the set before a gig means editing a list, not rewriting logic. That separation is a real software-design skill, and worth mentioning in your portfolio write-up.
  • goToTrack() applies a track's settings and triggers the transition and title card. You press N as the DJ mixes into the next track.
  • Two beat sources. Automatic detection works most of the time. When it struggles (busy breaks, ambient tracks, a noisy room), tap T on the beat 4 times and the engine switches to a steady tempo clock. Press G to go back to automatic.
  • Overlays are drawn in a fixed order: scene → title card → transition fade → strobe → blackout → HUD. Blackout always wins, so it's always safe.

Scene recipes for your own designs

Kaleidoscope

translate(width/2, height/2);
for (let k = 0; k < 8; k++) {
  push();
  rotate(TWO_PI * k / 8);
  if (k % 2) scale(1, -1);  // mirror
  drawSlice();              // anything!
  pop();
}

Pulsing typography

textAlign(CENTER, CENTER);
textStyle(BOLD);
textSize(min(width, height) * (0.15 + A.kick * 0.1));
fill(palHue(0), 80, 100);
text(SETLIST[track].title.toUpperCase(),
     width / 2, height / 2);

Barcode strobe

background(0);
noStroke();
randomSeed(A.beats);    // new pattern each beat
for (let x = 0; x < width; x += random(4, 40)) {
  fill(0, 0, random() < A.treble ? 100 : 0);
  rect(x, 0, random(2, 20), height);
}
