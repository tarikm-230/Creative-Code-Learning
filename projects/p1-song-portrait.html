Project 1 — Song Portrait

A visual made for one specific track. It knows where the intro, build, breakdown and drop are, and it changes with them. It plays start to finish like a music video that's generated live by code.

The brief

  • Pick one track your DJ friend loves to play (ideally one with a clear breakdown and drop)
  • Build a full-screen visual with three layers: base (slow mood), rhythm (beats) and detail (highs and texture)
  • It must follow the song's structure: each section looks clearly different, and the drop is the visual peak
  • Add a title card at the start and a fade to black at the end
  • Deliverable: a 30–90 second screen recording plus the exported HTML (lesson 11)
Time budget: about 5½ hours Work through the milestones in order and tick them off. They're saved in this browser. If you fall behind, skip the optional items, not the milestones. A simple portrait that follows the structure beats a complex one that doesn't.

Milestones

  1. Listen like a VJ (30 min). Load the track with the track bar. Listen 2–3 times with headphones. Write down: 3 mood words, the main instruments, and one visual idea ("the vocal feels like fog", "the kick is a heartbeat"). Pick a palette of 2–3 hues.
  2. Map the structure (20 min). Run the starter below, click to play, and press M each time a new section starts. Each press prints a ready-to-paste cue line in the console under the output. Paste them into CUES and rename them (intro, build, breakdown, drop, outro…).
  3. Style each section (30 min). Fill in STYLES: a hue and an energy level for each section name. Set TITLE. Use ← → to jump around and check every section looks different.
  4. Base layer (60 min). Replace or rework drawBase() with something that expresses your mood words. Use your noise blob, Unknown Pleasures, a tunnel or gradients. Drive it with slow values (A.mid, energy).
  5. Rhythm layer (60 min). Rework drawRhythm() so the beat is felt: rings, flashes, scale kicks, particle bursts. Use A.beat, A.kick and A.bass.
  6. Detail layer (45 min). Rework drawDetail(): waveforms, sparkles on hi-hats (A.treble), texture. Keep it subtle.
  7. The drop (30 min). Make the drop unforgettable: onSectionChange() already runs at every cue, so add a special case for 'drop'. Think contrast: if the breakdown is sparse and cool, the drop should be dense and hot.
  8. Polish (30 min). Title card typography, end fade, pixelDensity(1), test fullscreen (F), hide the HUD (H). Show it to your DJ friend and get one round of feedback.
  9. Ship it (15 min). ⤓ Export, test the exported file, then record it following lesson 11.

The starter

It works out of the box with the demo beat, and its cues already match the demo's sections. Click the output to play. Keys: Space/click play or pause · ← → seek 5 s · M mark a cue · H HUD · F fullscreen.

How the starter works

  • CUES lists section start times. Every frame, currentSection() finds the last cue whose start time has passed.
  • Edge detection (lesson 09): when the section name changes, onSectionChange(from, to) runs once. That's where one-off events go.
  • hueNow and energy glide toward the section's style instead of jumping. hueDist makes the hue take the short way round the color wheel (from 350 to 10 is +20, not -340).
  • Three layer functions, each wrapped in push()/pop(), are drawn back to front: base, then rhythm, then detail.
  • Seeking with the arrow keys lets you test the drop 20 times in a row without waiting.

Design advice

Contrast is everything

The drop only feels big if what came before it was small. Make breakdowns emptier than you think: fewer elements, less motion, darker colors. Then go all-in on the drop.

One idea, done well

Choose one visual motif (a circle, a horizon, a grid, a line) and let it evolve through the song. Throwing every effect in at once ends up looking like a screensaver.

Borrow from the track

Use its artwork colors, its title, a lyric, the genre's culture. Techno suits monochrome and geometry. Disco and house suit warmth and sparkle. Ambient suits slow, soft, noise-based visuals.

Map the right sound to the right thing

Kick → scale and flashes. Bass → weight and size. Mids and vocals → color and slow form. Hi-hats → sparkle and texture. When the mapping makes sense, the audience feels it without thinking about it.

Want hand-placed moments? Add more data to each cue, like { name: 'drop', start: 64.2, text: 'LET GO' }, and read cue.text when that section starts. You can also schedule things inside a section using t - cue.start (seconds since the section began). This is how music videos are made in code.
Stuck or behind schedule? The starter already counts as a valid portrait. Customize the CUES, STYLES and TITLE, swap drawBase() for one of your lesson challenges (Unknown Pleasures makes an excellent base layer), and move on to Project 2. Done beats perfect when the deadline is in hours.
